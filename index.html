The balanced accuracy p-value of 0.040 confirms that the sPLS-DA multi-omics model achieves statistically significant group discrimination beyond chance, despite the small sample size (n=28). The overall accuracy p-value (0.072) does not reach the conventional threshold — consistent with the imbalanced group sizes giving more weight to the larger PE and PTD groups in overall accuracy. Balanced accuracy is therefore the primary reported metric.

Key Findings

Multi-omics integration outperforms single-modality classification

Arm C The combined metabolomics + transcriptomics model (Scenario A) reached 53.0% balanced accuracy — significantly above chance (p = 0.040, permutation test). Gene-only (38.4%) and metabolite-only (41.4%) models were substantially weaker, confirming that the two modalities carry complementary discriminatory information.

PE and PTD are not well-separated from each other

Arm C Binary classification (Control vs Disease) reached 64.3% accuracy — far higher than 3-class performance. This suggests the molecular signatures of PE and PTD share common ground, and that the 3-class problem is the harder and more informative target.

Sphingolipids are the most robust metabolite signal

Arm C D-Sphingosine and Sphingosine (d18:1) show 100% ML stability across all 28 LOOCV folds and are PE-elevated (Log2FC ~+0.97). These are the strongest individual metabolite findings from the pipeline.

TUBA3D — KEGG co-annotation candidate for network analysis

Arm B TUBA3D shows nominal Spearman correlation with both sphingosines (ρ ≈ 0.59, p<0.001) and shares the Apoptosis pathway annotation (map04210) in KEGG. This co-annotation makes TUBA3D + sphingolipids a priority pair for validation by STRING or MetaboAnalyst Joint Pathway — not a demonstrated functional interaction from the current data. TUBA3D was not retained among the 38 ML-stable features.

PTD has a stronger univariate gene signal

Arm A 2.7× more genes reach nominal significance in PTD vs Control (294) than in PE vs Control (111). Top PTD-stable genes include DFFA (apoptosis), FNDC10, SNORA44, and several RN7SL RNA genes. No genes survived FDR correction in either comparison — consistent with n=28 power limits.

Limitations

Sample size constrains statistical power

n=28 is insufficient for genome-wide FDR correction across 315,576 correlation pairs or 4,383 differential expression tests. All results are exploratory and require validation in an independent cohort. The permutation test (p=0.040) confirms the ML model is non-random, but not its clinical utility.

Hyperparameter tuning on full dataset

keepX for sPLS-DA was tuned on all 28 samples before LOOCV prediction. This is an accepted approximation for n<30, but introduces a mild optimistic bias in the hyperparameter selection step. Accuracy and p-values are not affected (they come from the prediction loop only).

Pooled Spearman correlation

Gene–metabolite Spearman correlations were computed across all 28 samples pooled, not within groups. A correlation may reflect biological co-regulation or group composition effects. Within-group correlations would require n≥30 per group.

Feature discovery pipeline — not mechanistic inference

This pipeline identifies candidate features and KEGG co-annotation pairs. It does not establish functional interactions between genes and metabolites. Interpreting KEGG pathway overlap as evidence of direct biological interaction would overstate the findings. Mechanistic inference requires dedicated network tools (STRING protein interaction network, MetaboAnalyst Joint Pathway Analysis) applied to the candidate list as a planned next step.

Metabolomics Profile — Post-Engine Statistical Analysis

Everything described here occurs after the Metabolomics Engine (brain_engine.py), which performs structural/class-based quality annotation and assigns Exclusion_Reason = CLEAN and the Clnc_Endg_Cmpd flag. The 293 CLEAN compounds then enter the statistical profiling stage described below.

Filtering Pipeline

ACTIVE (28): Wilcoxon p < 0.05 OR |Log₂FC| ≥ 1.5 in at least one comparison
CLNC_FLAGGED (69): Clinically/endogenously relevant (Clnc_Endg_Cmpd = TRUE) — below statistical threshold, retained for biological context
BACKGROUND (196): Fails all filters · retained for reference

How the numbers connect

The 293 CLEAN metabolites split into three profiling categories: 28 ACTIVE (statistically significant), 69 CLNC_FLAGGED (clinically/endogenously relevant, below statistical threshold), and 196 BACKGROUND.
The 72-compound analytic input used in Arms B & C (Spearman correlation + sPLS-DA) is a separate cross-cutting filter: Clnc_Endg_Cmpd = TRUE OR p < 0.05 OR |Log₂FC| ≥ 2. It draws from both ACTIVE and CLNC_FLAGGED but is not identical to either — some ACTIVE compounds with only modest FC (1.5–2) and p ≥ 0.05 are excluded, while all 69 CLNC_FLAGGED enter by definition.
